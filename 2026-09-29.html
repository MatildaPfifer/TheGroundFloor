The short version

Capital got more expensive while Houston kept building. The 30-year mortgage average crossed 7% for the first time since January 2025, and in the same week Houston broke ground on the largest pharmaceutical ingredient plant in Texas history.

The pattern to notice is who is writing the big checks. Manufacturing is acting like the new anchor tenant: Eli Lilly in Generation Park, six Hines industrial projects driven by factory demand, and a speculative rail-served building going up next to Samsung in Taylor. Land near highways, rail and workforce is gaining value, and growth on the metro's edges, like Waller, starts to look like housing for the people who will staff these plants.

Houston homebuyers, meanwhile, have more choices than they have had in years. The rate jump takes back part of that leverage.

August home sales fall 11.5% as buyers gain the upper hand

Greater Houston closed about 7,100 single-family sales in August, down 11.5% from a year earlier. The median price slipped to $330,000 while the average rose 1.2% to $426,760, a sign that higher-priced homes are still moving. Inventory remains elevated, and apartment rents are down 1.2% year over year.

Capital marketsSept 24

Mortgage rates break 7% for the first time in 20 months

Freddie Mac's 30-year average rose to 7.03% from 6.95% a week earlier, and the 15-year climbed to 6.42%. A year ago the 30-year sat at 6.30%. The 10-year Treasury jumped to 5.11%, its highest in 19 years, pointing to continued pressure on both home loans and commercial debt.

McCord's business park along Beltway 8 in northeast Harris County just landed Eli Lilly's $6.5 billion active pharmaceutical ingredient plant on 236 acres. The facility will make orforglipron, Lilly's oral GLP-1 medicine, and should bring more than 4,000 construction jobs. It follows Bristol Myers Squibb's $2.3 billion plant announced there in August.

The lesson for developers is that large, entitled, infrastructure-ready land wins mega-projects. When a company needs to move fast, a site that is ready beats a site that is cheaper. Lilly is also funding training at San Jacinto College's new biotech center on the campus, which answers the workforce question at the same time.

Realty News Report

Underwriting desk: stress-test your exit

When the 10-year moves this much in a week, the assumption most likely to be wrong in a model is the exit cap rate. Before you trust a deal you are reviewing for class or a case competition:

  1. Rerun the model with the exit cap 50 and 100 basis points higher than the base case.
  2. Check whether the deal still clears its target IRR, or only works if rates come back down.
  3. Look at the debt side too. A higher rate can break the debt service coverage test before it breaks returns.
  4. Write one sentence on what would have to be true for the base case to hold.

If the deal only works when rates fall, you are making a rate bet, not a real estate bet. That is fine, as long as you know it.
