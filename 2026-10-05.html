The short version

Rates climbed again and hiring stalled, but the money kept moving. The 30-year mortgage average hit 7.28%, its sixth straight weekly increase, and the September jobs report came in far below forecasts.

The pattern this week is that buyers are paying for income they can see today. Interra bought two Galleria office towers that are nearly 90% leased at a basis its broker called well below replacement cost. Brixmor and Everview agreed to take a $2.3 billion grocery-anchored REIT private, and Houston's Partners Capital is closing in on $1 billion of deals after buying three more neighborhood centers. When debt is expensive, in-place cash flow is what buyers want to own.

On the housing side, developers are still betting on the metro's edges. A 3,400-home community is coming to Brazoria County, and Bridgeland opened its first 55-and-up neighborhood.

A 3,400-home community is coming to Brazoria County

IHP Capital Partners and Scarborough Lane Development are developing Peachtree on 1,458 acres near Alvin and Liverpool, about 28 miles south of downtown and five miles east of Highway 288. The first phase of 206 acres and 535 lots has closed, with first finished lots expected for builders in mid-2027. Plans include retail, land for two elementary schools and a lazy river.

Capital marketsOct 1

Mortgage rates rise for a sixth straight week to 7.28%

Freddie Mac's 30-year average jumped a full quarter point from 7.03% the week before, reaching its highest level since October 2023. The 15-year rose to 6.60%. A year ago, the 30-year averaged 6.34%.

EconomyOct 2

September hiring misses badly, which may keep the Fed on hold

Employers added just 29,000 jobs against forecasts of 84,000, unemployment ticked up to 4.2%, and revisions cut 60,000 jobs from July and August. Construction added 11,000 jobs. Markets rallied as traders bet the Fed will hold rates steady at its meeting later this month, which could take some pressure off borrowing costs.

RetailSept 28

Brixmor and Everview take a $2.3 billion grocery REIT private

The pair agreed to buy Toronto-listed Slate Grocery REIT for $13 a unit in cash, a roughly 20% premium. Brixmor will take 23 centers for $636 million, and a joint venture with Everview will hold the other 92 for $1.71 billion. Grocery-anchored retail keeps drawing institutional money because the tenants are needs-based and the cash flow is steady.

Interra Capital Group closed on two 18-story towers at 1233 and 1333 West Loop South. They were built in 1972 and heavily renovated between 2016 and 2022. The price was not disclosed, but Real Estate Alert reported it at about $110 million. JLL represented the seller, Regent Properties, and arranged financing through Morgan Stanley.

The lesson is in how the deal was pitched: strong occupancy, a weighted average lease term of 6.3 years, and a basis well below what it would cost to build new. That combination is what gets financed in a 7% world. JLL also noted that Houston has seen 10 office sales above $100 million in the last 18 months, a sign that large office trades are coming back for the right buildings.

Realty News Report

Underwriting desk: test the "below replacement cost" claim

Almost every offering memo says the asset trades below replacement cost. Here is how to check whether that actually protects you:

  1. Estimate what a comparable new building would cost per square foot, including land, hard costs, soft costs and developer profit.
  2. Add the capital you will need for leasing, tenant improvements and deferred maintenance to the purchase price. That is your real basis.
  3. Ask what rent a new building would need to justify construction. If market rents are well below that number, new supply is unlikely, and that is the real protection.
  4. Look at lease rollover. A cheap basis does not help much if a large share of tenants can leave in the next two years.

Below replacement cost is a starting point, not a thesis. It matters only if it keeps competitors from building next door.
