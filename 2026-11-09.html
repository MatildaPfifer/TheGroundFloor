The short version

It is officially a buyer's market. Redfin says the U.S. has more sellers than buyers by the widest margin on record, and Houston has the third-biggest surplus of sellers in the country.

Rates gave buyers a little more help. Bond yields slid after the election and a tech stock selloff, and Zillow's 30-year rate dipped below 7%. The catch is the job market: employers added 165,000 jobs in October, well above forecasts, which makes a December Fed hike more likely and could push rates back up.

Rentals are moving the other way. Houston apartment occupancy rose to 91.2%, and Hunington broke ground on 279 units in Katy. Downtown, the fallout from the Kirby Mansion demolition sparked a call for a city adaptive reuse czar, and the Astros announced a new garage.

Houston ranks third among the country's strongest buyer's markets

Redfin found that Miami, Nashville and Houston had the biggest surplus of sellers over buyers for a second straight month. Houston inventory rose to 5.5 months of supply in September from 5.1 a year earlier, close to the six months HAR economist Ted C. Jones calls balanced, and homes took an average of 58 days to sell.

Beyond Houston

EconomyNov 6

A strong jobs report puts a December hike back in play

Employers added 165,000 jobs in October, far more than economists expected, and unemployment edged down to 4.1%. The Fed tends to raise rates only when the labor market can handle it, so the report makes a hike at the December 8 to 9 meeting look more likely. October's inflation data on November 10 is the next big test.

Mortgage rates slip below 7% in daily data

Zillow's average 30-year rate fell to 6.96% on November 6, down 11 basis points from a week earlier, as a tech stock selloff pushed investors into bonds. The 10-year Treasury closed at 4.90% on November 4, down 15 basis points over the week. A hot inflation report could reverse the slide quickly.

HousingNov 7

The U.S. has the fewest homebuyers on record

Redfin counted about 929,000 active buyers in September, the lowest since its records began in 2013, against about 1.5 million sellers, the most since March 2020. Forty-one of the 50 largest metros are buyer's markets. Only five are seller's markets, including San Francisco, where AI wealth is driving competition.

The day after the Kirby Mansion came down, Bisnow hosted a downtown conference on conversions and adaptive reuse. Ryan LeVasseur of LeVass Ventures proposed that the city appoint an adaptive reuse czar to save historic buildings before they reach the wrecking ball. The city's chief economic development officer, Gwendolyn Tillotson-Bell, said her office was brought into the Kirby situation too late and supported the idea.

The Astros said they will break ground in December on a garage north of Daikin Park, to be finished before the 2028 season, and are interested in a hotel and entertainment district nearby. Speakers agreed a district likely waits until the George R. Brown Convention Center expansion is done in 2028. On housing, LeVasseur noted downtown rents have stayed roughly flat since about 5,000 units were built, while construction costs are up 40% to 50%.

The lesson is that older buildings only survive when someone can make the numbers work. Conversions like 1031 Main need a low purchase price, a building layout that fits apartments, and often city help. A dedicated point person at City Hall could make those deals happen earlier, before demolition becomes the easy answer.

Realty News Report

Underwriting desk: does an office conversion pencil?

With older downtown offices sitting 25% to 40% vacant, conversions keep coming up. Here is a quick way to screen one:

  1. Check the floor plate. Apartments need windows, so very deep floors leave dark interior space that is hard to use. Narrower towers convert more easily.
  2. Find the plumbing. Every unit needs kitchens and bathrooms, and adding new plumbing lines through a concrete tower is one of the biggest costs.
  3. Work backward from rent. If downtown rents are flat and construction costs are up 40% to 50%, the purchase price has to be very low for the deal to work.
  4. Count the help. Tax abatements, grants or historic tax credits often close the gap, so find out what the city and state offer before you make an offer.

Most conversions are won or lost on the purchase price. Buy the building cheaply enough, and the rest becomes a construction problem instead of a financing problem.
