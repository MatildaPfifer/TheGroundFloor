The week in one read

Capital got more expensive while Houston kept building. The 30-year mortgage average crossed 7% for the first time since January 2025 as Treasury yields climbed, and in the same week Houston broke ground on the largest pharmaceutical ingredient plant in Texas history.

Houston buyers now have more homes to choose from than they have seen in years, and prices have softened a bit at the median. The rate jump takes back part of that leverage. On the commercial side, manufacturing and logistics tenants continue to drive the biggest deals in the region.

Hallway takeaway

The question this fall is shifting from "where is the demand?" to "does my deal still work at today's cost of capital?" Houston has the demand story. Financing is now the swing factor.

Trend to watch: manufacturing is the new anchor tenant

Look at where the biggest checks landed this month. Eli Lilly started work on a $6.5 billion plant in northeast Houston. Hines announced six industrial projects totaling more than 2.3 million square feet, with manufacturing demand as the stated driver. Houston-based Partners Real Estate is building a 572,640-square-foot speculative, rail-served building near Samsung's plant in Taylor.

For Houston, that means land near highways, rail and workforce is getting more valuable, and the fast growth on the edges of the metro, like Waller, starts to make more sense as housing for the people who will staff these plants.

McCord's business park along Beltway 8 in northeast Harris County just landed Eli Lilly's $6.5 billion active pharmaceutical ingredient plant on 236 acres. Realty News Report

Analyst's corner: stress-test your exit

When the 10-year moves this much in a week, the assumption most likely to be wrong in a model is the exit cap rate. Before you trust any deal you are reviewing for class or a competition:

  1. Rerun the model with the exit cap 50 and 100 basis points higher than the base case.
  2. Check whether the deal still clears its target IRR, or only works if rates come back down.
  3. Look at the debt side too. A higher rate can break the debt service coverage test before it breaks returns.
  4. Write one sentence on what would have to be true for the base case to hold.

If the deal only works when rates fall, you are making a rate bet, not a real estate bet. That is fine, as long as you know it.
