The short version

Mortgage rates eased for two straight weeks to 7.24%, the Fed held steady in a split vote, and Houston just recorded the biggest industrial campus sale in its history.

Industrial remains Houston's strongest story. A 64-building, crane-served park in northwest Houston traded to a joint venture of Adkisson Group and PCCP, a southeast Houston warehouse project sold ahead of schedule after an unsolicited offer, and new construction financing closed in north Houston. Investors are paying up for buildings that are hard to replicate.

The industry also gave Houston a strong vote of confidence. ULI and PwC's annual Emerging Trends survey ranked Houston first in the country for homebuilding prospects in 2027 and sixth overall. Meanwhile, the demolition of the 132-year-old Kirby Mansion in Midtown was a reminder that Houston's lack of zoning cuts both ways.

Housing was also on the ballot in Tuesday's midterms. Florida voters approved a major homestead tax break, California approved new housing bonds and down-payment help, and San Francisco voted on several real estate tax measures. Early results are below.

OutlookNov 2

Houston ranks first for homebuilding in ULI and PwC's 2027 outlook

The Emerging Trends in Real Estate report, based on input from nearly 1,500 investors, developers, lenders and advisors, put Houston at the top of 81 markets for homebuilding prospects and sixth overall. PwC's Andrew Alperstein credited Houston's more diversified economy, its port, energy and industrial demand, and low barriers to entry. Houston ranked first overall in 2015, then fell to 30th after the oil bust.

The historic Kirby Mansion is demolished in Midtown

After 132 years, lumber baron John Henry Kirby's mansion at 2000 Smith is gone. Demolition started in late October, was halted by the city over permits, then resumed and finished on November 2. The buyer has ties to a convenience store and gas station chain, though a gas station has not been confirmed. The city is separately proposing new rules on where gas stations can be built.

Beyond Houston

Capital marketsOct 29

Mortgage rates ease for a second straight week

Freddie Mac's 30-year average fell to 7.24% from 7.32% the week before, which itself was down from 7.40%. It is a modest break after seven straight weekly increases through early October, but rates remain near their highest level since late 2023.

RateZip via AOL

EconomyOct 28

The Fed holds rates steady, but the vote was split

The Federal Reserve kept its target range at 3.75% to 4% in a 10 to 2 vote, after raising rates in September. Markets have started pricing in a possible hike at the next meeting on December 9, when the Fed will also release new projections.

HousingNov 2

Homeowners with 3% mortgages are still not selling

Realtor.com's latest mortgage data shows 19.2% of outstanding mortgages carry a rate below 3%, and loans taken out between mid-2019 and mid-2022 now make up a record 42.8% of the market. Only 27.8% of outstanding loans were originated after rates began rising in mid-2022. The lock-in is easing, but slowly, which keeps resale inventory tight.

Midterms: housing on the ballot

Real estate measures from the November 3 election, based on early and unofficial results as of November 4. Counts may change.

Property taxFlorida

Florida approves a much bigger homestead exemption

Amendment 3 passed with broad support. The share of a primary home's value exempt from non-school property taxes will rise from $50,000 to $150,000 in 2027, then to $250,000 in 2028, which should cut many homeowners' annual tax bills significantly. Watch for how local governments make up the lost revenue.

WUNC via Quartz

Housing financeCalifornia

California backs housing bonds and down-payment help

Voters approved Proposition 1, which lets the state borrow $11.25 billion for housing, mostly rental construction and renovation with set-asides for affordable and supportive housing. They also approved Proposition 37, which creates a fund offering down-payment loans to middle-class buyers that are repaid to the state over time.

CalMattersWUNC via Quartz

Transfer taxesSan Francisco

San Francisco votes on real estate taxes and affordable housing

A measure to more than double city funding for a key affordable housing fund passed with 80% of the vote, and a measure ending a partial transfer tax exemption on some foreclosures passed with 79%. Proposition I, which would steer half of the tax on property sales above $10 million to affordable and social housing, was trailing with 45% in early returns.

Fair housingAkron, Ohio

Akron passes two housing access measures

Issue 12, which treats criminal history as a protected class in housing, passed narrowly, 51% to 49%, in unofficial results, and Issue 11 also passed. Real estate associations, the local chamber and the mayor opposed both, and opponents have said they plan to sue. City Council now has to write the legislation.

The Texas Development Company sold this roughly 230-acre campus near Highway 290 and the Sam Houston Tollway to a joint venture of Adkisson Group and PCCP. Stream Realty Partners says it is the largest known industrial campus sale in Greater Houston by both square footage and acreage. Stream's Jon Farris represented the seller and will lead leasing, and the late Travis Land of Partners Real Estate represented the buyer.

The lesson is in what makes the park hard to copy. Every building has bridge cranes rated from 5 to 75 tons, the campus has a Union Pacific rail spur, heavy power and large yards, and it sits outside both the 100-year and 500-year floodplains. Farris said demand for crane-served space in Houston has never been higher. Features like these take years and serious money to build, which is why tenants stay and buyers compete for them.

Realty News Report

Underwriting desk: valuing a hard-to-replicate asset

Brokers love calling a property "one of one." Here is how to test whether the special features actually support the price:

  1. Price the features separately. Estimate what it would cost today to add cranes, extra power and rail access to a standard building. That premium is what you are really paying for.
  2. Check the tenant pool. Specialized buildings attract fewer tenants, but those tenants are stickier because moving heavy equipment is expensive. Look at renewal history, not just occupancy.
  3. Count the competition. If no one can easily build the same thing nearby, rents have room to grow. If a new crane-served park is on the drawing board, the premium shrinks.
  4. Do not skip the site basics. Floodplain status, insurance cost and road access can matter as much as the buildings themselves in Houston.

Scarcity is only valuable if tenants will pay for it. Make the seller prove it with lease data.
